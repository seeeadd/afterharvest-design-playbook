# Jason Fladlien on pages: how his advice is used, and new ideas

Purpose: give every page job one place to look up Jason Fladlien's design and selling rules, see exactly how they
were turned into web page parts on Ana Calin's registration and VIP pages, know where they stop applying, and pick
new ideas to try. The file gets better with every job (section 7).

Related files: [page-sop.md](page-sop.md) (how a job runs and the judge checklist), [registration-page.md](registration-page.md),
[vip-page.md](vip-page.md), [ui-kit.md](ui-kit.md), [layout-type-spacing.md](layout-type-spacing.md), [copy.md](copy.md),
[motion-and-speed.md](motion-and-speed.md), [evolution-ana.md](evolution-ana.md). House rules it sits under:
`../README.md`, `../SOP.md`, `../graphics.md` (real vs painted, no image generators), `../motion.md` (banned motion),
`../video-editing.md` (Fladlien in the video), `../clients/ana-calin.md` (her facts), `../feedback-log.md`.

## 0. How to use this file

- **Sources.** The study repo is `seeeadd/fladdy`. Read, in this order: `TEARDOWNS.md`, then
  `jason-fladlien-videos/01 How to Design Webinar Slides Nobody Does This Right (rAwgv2hThVw)/teardown.md` (the main
  one, the design video), then 02, 03 and 04, then `rules/slide-method.md` and `playbooks/slides.md`. For exact
  wording check `transcript.md` (paragraph stamps) and `distinct.md`; for what was on screen, `study.md` and the
  frame `sheets/`.
- **Stamps in this file.** "V01 1:55" means video 01 at 1:55, as stamped in that video's teardown (taken from the
  caption file; the paragraph stamps in `transcript.md` can sit a few seconds earlier). V01 is the slide design video,
  V02 "The New Webinar", V03 "This is the New Way to Sell", V04 "AI is (mostly) Bullshit". Points marked "playbook"
  come from `fladdy/playbooks/*.md`, which mix Jason's words with a methodology doc's notes: treat them as guides.
- **Before designing any page:** Sean wants the teardown studied as the foundation, not skimmed. Sean (Nov 1): "go
  through the video teardown of Fladlien in the repository. Not just the teardown, but the transcript and everything,
  the video frames ... use it as a foundation for designing anything." For presentation-style graphics (slides,
  screen-shares inside a page) also follow the factory's slide guideline
  (`outreach-thumbnail-factory/prompts/4-day1-slide.md`) and Sean's motion study (`tools/paint/MOTION.md` in the client
  repo). Sean: "I want you to use the presentation guideline on the outreach thumbnail factory ... make sure the
  animations are also premium and are following the lessons to study in the motion graphics that I sent earlier."
- **What wins when rules clash:** Sean's rulings and approvals, then the client's facts and approved copy, then
  Jason. Jason's rules are a filter for function; Sean decides the look (section 4).
- **Apply it from the page's goal down.** Write the page's one goal first (registration: the free seat; VIP: the
  visitor picks VIP on their own). Every Fladlien device must serve that goal. Sean (Nov 1): "I don't want you to get
  distracted. I want you to always look into the goal of the page."
- **Placement decisions are the Fladlien layer; the look is a separate layer.** Sean approved where things go before
  he approved how they look: "I liked the areas where you put the design, like the decisions are right. Now we have
  to elevate the design". So: decide placement with Jason's rules first, then raise the look with `../graphics.md`
  and never move a placement Sean approved while doing it.

## 1. The ten rules in one list

1. Ask "how do we show this?" of every line; answer with the real thing (V01 37:09, 20:05).
2. The most important words are the biggest; the price, titles, names and numbers go small (V01 1:55, 41:22).
3. One idea per band, built one item at a time, in reading order, as it is read (V01 6:09, 21:18).
4. Animation only to point the eye at the next thing; keep the page calm (V01 21:18, Sean's banned list).
5. Bring the framework back with progress: done ticked and small, now big, next faded (V01 24:35).
6. Show the plan three times with the same labels (V01 3:37).
7. Tease with real slides and leave a loop open (V01 2:58, 4:07).
8. Build tables row by row (V01 29:59); show numbers as countable things (V01 9:39), in the real unit.
9. Extend one visual across the section or the funnel instead of switching pictures (V01 18:42).
10. Close with his call to action layout: what you get, the total big, the price small, one action impossible to
    miss (V01 41:47).

## 2. Each principle, on a page

Each entry: Jason's words, what it means on a web page, where it is on Ana's pages, how to do it for the next client.

### 2.1 Show, don't tell: "How do we show this?"

- **Jason:** "'How do we show this?' This is always the question I ask myself when I design slides." (V01 37:09).
  "I didn't say you post. I showed the post from LinkedIn." (V01 17:35). Show not tell is "A principle I'm going to
  teach you right away that will completely shift all of your design if you get it" (V01 2:08).
- **On a page:** every claim next to a picture of the thing itself: the screen, the message, the document, the
  calendar. Write the line, the belief it must leave, and how it is shown, before picking a layout
  (`fladdy/rules/slide-method.md` section 1).
- **Ana, VIP:** each VIP part is shown working, never described: the live review (a draft on a painted laptop marked
  by her pen as it plays, beside a Zoom-style Q&A list), the WhatsApp chat that plays message by message, the Offer Clarity Sprint as a real course
  screenshot on a painted laptop with the real mockup beside it, the recordings on a player. The spam note at the top
  plays the Gmail demo on its left (`.vspam`, `.vspam-demo`): Spam, "Report not spam", Inbox.
- **Ana, registration:** each Day row plays the day's own slides as a live screen share (`.lv`, the tag "From the
  Day 1 slides"); the host's numbers are a Google Ads style overview (`.hrc.gads`), not a sentence; the closing
  "See you Tuesday" is the real Google Calendar invite in her colours (`.gi`).
- **Concepts are proposed this way first.** For each picture: the line it sits by, the belief ("by Friday I'll have
  something real to send"), the object. Ana's VIP concepts (Nov 1, 15:08) each started "from Jason's question, 'how
  do we show this?'". Sean: "I love the ideas".
- **Next client:** list every section's key line, answer "how do we show this?" with something from the client's
  real work (their inbox, their course, their calendar, their students' notes). Only when nothing real exists,
  paint a metaphor (2.2).

### 2.2 Real where it proves, painted where it is a feeling

- **Jason:** "We want to show it in the real world visually as much as possible. Not metaphorically, not
  conceptually, not with AI generated images. Screenshots are your best friend on a webinar." (V01 20:05). "So, we
  only go to stock when we really don't have anything else" (V01 9:07). For a statistic: "I go to the actual source
  and I screenshot it. I still put the source underneath of it. I highlight the key part" (V01 20:43).
- **On a page (Claude's split on Nov 1, 15:11, which Sean took with "go ahead"):** anything that proves what they get must be the real thing; anything that
  carries a feeling, or a process that has not happened yet, can be a painting. Best of both: the painted object is
  the stage and the real screen sits inside it, sharp and unpainted. Sean asked for it: "in some areas that require
  more specificity and raw stuff other than metaphor, we can start to create the raw stuff ... You tell me which stuff
  has to be raw and which stuff is good as metaphors or just visuals." The full rule is `../graphics.md`.
- **The source line:** under each real screen, a small caption naming where it is from. Ana VIP: `.src` "From the
  Offer Clarity Sprint" (serif italic 15px, `--ink3` `#7A6A60`); registration: `.lv-tag` "From the
  Day 1 slides" (and Day 2, Day 3). Proof cards carry their label too ("A student in my past challenge", "From
  Jill's experience working with Ana. Individual results vary.").
- **Recreations carry no label at all.** Sean removed the "Example" stamps from recreated screens ("can you remove
  the example annotation"). So: a source line goes only under a real screen; a recreation is swapped for the real
  screen when the client sends it, and is never presented as the real thing.
- **Real frames for people, not logos or names alone.** Proof that the host knows people in her field is a real
  frame from their call; a guest with no real frame is left out. Sean: "i kinda wanna show these instead like the
  actual video frames" and "maybe not have to include kristina coz we don have vid of her". Logos of their products
  were declined (they imply a partnership).
- **Highlight the key part only:** one key line per section in `mark.sk` (bold, a sunflower band swept in as it
  arrives); see 2.6.
- **A raw look beats a rebuilt one.** Sean on the host numbers card: "I want that to look a little bit more like a
  raw screenshot instead of something like this. It looks Claude-generated if we keep it like that." It became the
  Google Ads overview (`.hrc.gads`: four scorecards in Google's colours, `#4285F4`, `#DB4437`, `#F4B400`, `#0F9D58`,
  First sales 2, Readers 80K, Revenue $1.4M, Sales calls 0, a two-line chart from "First workshop" to "Today").
- **Next client:** sort every picture into "must be real" and "can be painted" and send the list to Sean before
  painting. Ask the client for the real screenshots; until they come, build an exact recreation (no "Example" stamp,
  see above), list it for Sean as a recreation, and swap it later. Never present a recreation as the real thing.

### 2.3 The most important words are the biggest

- **Jason:** "they make the least important thing the biggest thing and therefore the center and focus of attention
  when it should be the other way around." (V01 1:55). The usual culprits: the title (V01 13:18), the presenter's name
  (V01 11:31), numbers (V01 27:40), and the price: "$10,000 in a giant font seems more expensive than $10,000 in a
  smaller font." (V01 41:22).
- **On a page:** size by importance to the decision, not by habit. Section eyebrows small; the promise or the
  package big; the price modest; step numbers tiny.
- **Ana, VIP order card (`.order`):** the label "VIP · one payment" with the price `$67` at 20px (`.oh .op`), the
  heading "Get the full *VIP package.*" at 34px (`.order h2`), and the four package lines with their painted icons
  (`.stack li`, each with its painted icon in a 48px column) as the visual focus. The buy box price is serif weight 400 at 56px
  (`.vb .bb .price`, commented "the price not giant (Jason 41:22)").
- **Ana, VIP numbering:** item numbers are small ("Nº 01" in the rail, `.num` 03 on the item), never the hero of
  the band.
- **Next client:** in every card that holds a price, make what they get the largest element and keep the price at
  about the body size of the card heading's smaller half. Check every section: is the biggest thing on screen the
  thing that moves them? If it is a label, shrink it.

### 2.4 Inside one line, the less important half is smaller

- **Jason:** "Even though it's one sentence, I'm making it as easy as possible for the audience to take in."
  (V01 7:55 to 8:06), about setting the less important half of a line smaller, same font.
- **Ana, every registration button:** the action words full size, the free tail smaller: `small.bq` at `.8em`,
  weight 600, opacity `.85` ("Show me where to start (it's free)", "Show me your numbers, Ana (free)"). The code
  still drops the tail on the phone bar under 390px (`.mbar .btn .bq{display:none}`); that breaks Sean's "Don't
  remove for free." and is a still-to-do: tighten the bar instead ([ui-kit.md](ui-kit.md) section 10). Rule kept in
  [copy.md](copy.md) section 7 and [ui-kit.md](ui-kit.md) section 11.
- **Ana, Day labels:** the visitor's own time big, New York in grey beside it (`.dny` 13.5px, weight 600).
- **Next client:** in buttons, labels and stat lines, split the line into "what it is" and "the detail"; set the
  detail at about 0.8em and lighter.

### 2.5 Bigger type for the people who buy

- **Jason:** "Bigger fonts are better than smaller fonts functionally." (V01 14:02); the best buyers are "30, 40, 50
  plus" (V01 14:17).
- **Ana:** registration Day bullets `.dtext li{font-size:17px}` and the Days lede `.days .dlede{font-size:19px}`
  (comment "Jason: bigger type reads better for the people who buy (30, 40, 50 plus)"); the VIP page sets
  `body{font-size:18px}`.
- **Next client:** body 17 to 18px on desktop, no reading text under 15px on phones except labels and legal lines;
  check at the client's real audience age.

### 2.6 One idea per band, built one item at a time

- **Jason:** "Slides are free. So, we should use as many of them as we possibly can." (V01 6:09). A list that must
  stay on screen gets its highlight animated instead: bullet one, two, three appear in turn, or "animate the
  highlight behind it" (V01 12:10 to 12:14).
- **On a page:** bands are not free (a page has a length), so the rule becomes: one idea per band, one focal picture
  per band, and the items inside appear in reading order as they come into view. See
  [motion-and-speed.md](motion-and-speed.md) section 2 ("One moment per section").
- **Ana, VIP:** "What's in VIP" is one band per part (`.it` 01 to 04, alternating sides with `.flip`).
- **Ana, both pages:** the moving highlight is `mark.sk`: bold ink, a sunflower band `rgba(255,212,0,.6)` from 52% to
  94% of the line, swept from 0 to 100% width over 1.2s `cubic-bezier(.22,.8,.24,1)` when it arrives. The Day
  headlines use a lighter band on the key phrase only (`.dtext h3 .dh`, 42% sunflower from 58% to 90%).
- **Ana, registration:** one row per day (`.drow`), the slides window beside the bullets.
- **Next client:** if a band holds two ideas, split it or cut one. In each band, choose the one line that gets the
  highlight; never highlight two.

### 2.7 Motion only to lead the eye; keep it calm

- **Jason:** "The beauty of animation isn't because it looks cool. It's because it can focus the attention on the
  information sequentially." (V01 21:18). "I'm a huge fan of functional animation." (V01 9:25). He has motion "every
  six seconds" on slides (V01 28:52).
- **On a page:** each section plays one small story when it is seen, and the motion points at the next thing
  (the button, the next row, the next day). Nothing moves to decorate.
- **Ana, VIP:** the button shimmer was removed in the Fladlien redesign (`.btn:after{animation:none;display:none}`,
  commented "no pulsing CTA: calm reads as confidence"). The free seat is shown complete and good, so people choose
  VIP themselves. Every later round kept it: nothing on the buy path pulses or nags.
- **Ana, VIP:** a pencil arrow draws itself from the spam note down to the offer (Sean asked for "an arrow animation
  on there to make them scroll to the VIP page so it becomes the focus").
- **Next client:** for every animation ask "what does this point at?" If the answer is "nothing", cut it. How it
  moves (arcs, anticipation, settle, speed) is `../motion.md` and [motion-and-speed.md](motion-and-speed.md).

### 2.8 The mechanism comes back with progress

- **Jason:** on the framework slide, "I'll check it, make it smaller, show the second point bigger that we're
  covering now. And then the third point is tease. I made it a little opaque" (V01 24:35); "So now they always know
  where they're at. They always know where they're going." (V01 24:56). The mechanism slides are "the most important
  slides" (V01 23:04).
- **On a page:** a slim tracker that stays on screen while the visitor reads the steps: finished steps ticked and
  small, the current one big and in the accent, the next faded.
- **Ana, registration, Day 1 to 3 tracker (`.dmap`):** sticky (`top:88px`; desktop 1061px and wider sits top right,
  `justify-content:flex-end;top:96px`, scaled `.82`; phones `top:8px`). A white pill with a 1.6px ink border.
  Current day `.dm-s.cur`: full opacity, a pink `#F72585` circle scaled 1.25, label 18px, "Day 1" in pink. Done
  `.dm-s.done`: ink circle with a white tick, label 12.5px struck through. Next: opacity `.36`. Labels "Your people",
  "What to sell", "The path". Under 400px only the current label shows. Sean placed it: "can you put this floating
  thing for days 1 to 3 under the top-right side instead".
- **Ana, VIP, the index rail (`.rail`):** one sticky rail beside "What's in VIP" with the four part icons and "Nº 01"
  to "Nº 04": the part in view lit (`.on`, full size), done parts `.75` opacity, the rest `.38` and scaled `.86`.
  Wide screens only (1360px and wider).
- **Ana, VIP header:** the three steps "Seat saved" (done, ticked), "VIP upgrade" (now, 2), then the last step, in
  the floating bar ("Step 2 of 3").
- **Reversal that teaches:** the first redesign repeated a four-stamp strip over every VIP item (`.mst`, "done ticked
  and small, now big, next faded (Jason 24:35)"). Sean then said "The UI right now is a little bit generic-ish and
  repetitive", and it became the one rail. Lesson: show the mechanism once, in one sticky place, with progress; do
  not stamp a copy of it on every section.
- **Next client:** find the event's own steps (days, weeks, phases) and build one tracker in their accent. Labels are
  the step names from the copy, two or three words each.

### 2.9 Show the plan three times, with the same labels

- **Jason:** "The first time somebody sees it, they're likely to forget it." Show it in the intro, in the teaching,
  and in the recap: "Seeing it in three different ways from three different angles and three different perspectives
  massively increases the buyin." (V01 3:37 to 3:58).
- **On a page:** the plan appears near the top, through the middle, and at the close, with identical labels. Each
  time from a new angle (a thumbnail, a tracker, a recap), never the same block pasted three times.
- **Ana, registration:** (1) the hero booking strip shows the Day 1 slide as a mini (`.book .mini`, alt "Day 1:
  where I'd find my first readers from zero"); (2) the slide wall and the tracker through the days (`.swall`,
  `.dmap`: "Your people / What to sell / The path"); (3) the closing recap thumbnails (`.recap3`: "Day 1 · your
  people", "Day 2 · what to sell", "Day 3 · the path"). When a label changes, rename it in all three places.
- **Ana, VIP:** the four part icons (`mini-rec`, `mini-rev`, `mini-wa`, `mini-spr`) recur in the order card, the
  table, the rail and the buy box: the same four things seen from four angles.
- **Next client:** write the three labels once in the copy doc, then grep the page for every place they appear
  before each publish.

### 2.10 Slide inside a slide, and the open loop

- **Jason:** "So much of what you can do in the introduction to tease an audience is to just literally show a slide
  inside of a slide." (V01 2:58). One slide of "all of the slides that I could fit on the screen", some cut off "at
  the very bottom. So you only see half of them." (V01 4:07 to 4:38). "But we use words too much and we use visuals
  too little." (V01 4:19).
- **Ana, registration slide wall (`.swall`):** under the Days heading, a pill "From my slides for the three days"
  over a 3-column grid of her six slides, 16px gap, `max-height:300px`, faded out by a mask (`#000 58%` to transparent
  at 96%), so the second row is cut off. Odd slides tilt -1deg, even +1deg; they stagger in on scroll (rise 26px,
  0.6s, 0.08s apart).
- **Ana, Day windows:** the slides are teasers, not title slides: the method under black bars and lock stickers,
  only words from the approved copy, mock-ups labelled. Sean: "I want to put the real slides, real-ish, something
  built to intrigue people, secret sauce." Details in [registration-page.md](registration-page.md) section 7.6.
- **Ana, VIP:** the real Sprint screenshots with the dollar-figure lesson titles hidden under mosaic: proof that it
  exists, the rest kept for buyers (Claude's note at the time: "Jason's open loop").
- **Next client:** build the event's real slides (or real course screens) first, then reuse them as the wall, the
  day windows and the recap. Show enough to prove it exists; lock the how.

### 2.11 Tables, built row by row

- **Jason:** "Tables are great at communicating information, especially when you animate them." (V01 29:59).
- **Ana, VIP comparison (`.cmp`):** "Your free seat and *VIP,* side by side". Rows rise 10px and fade in one by one
  (`transition-delay: calc(var(--i) * .14s + .3s)`), free rows first, then the four VIP rows (`tr.add2`) light up
  with their painted icons (`.stp`, 34px, tilted -3deg). The VIP column is framed like a ledger column: pink header
  text with a 2px pink rule, thin side rules `rgba(236,52,134,.28)`, a stronger bottom rule `rgba(236,52,134,.6)`, a
  light tint `rgba(247,37,133,.1)`. The free column says "no" for VIP-only rows, never a red cross (the free seat is
  not wrong), including "Lifetime workshop recordings". CTA under it: "Give me the VIP column ($67)".
- **Old vs new as a table (V02 frame 6:35; V03 2:38 to 12:26):** the host's then/now record on the registration page
  started as a row-by-row table on her photo and is now the Google Ads overview (2.2); the code comment still names
  the idea ("then and now (Jason: an old-vs-new table that grows row by row)").
- **Next client:** any "free vs paid", "before vs after" or "old way vs new way" goes in a table that builds row by
  row, the paid or new column framed in the accent, the other column honest and never shamed.

### 2.12 Make numbers countable, in the real unit

- **Jason:** "People don't understand numbers very well ... if I tell you think of 30, you have trouble thinking of
  it." (V01 9:39). 30 houses as 30 house icons, 350 build hours as 350 clocks (V01 35:42), 12 sessions on a calendar
  (V01 37:09).
- **Sean's correction (Nov 2):** the first version drew the small lists as dots (300, 547, 198 readers). Sean: "I
  don't know if they make sense here. I know this is Jason Fladlien's advice, but I am not sure if the execution is
  right. Maybe make them more excited about it." Rule: count the thing that excites (the money earned), in a form
  people recognise, not abstract marks.
- **Ana, registration results (`.trr-cash`):** one $100 note per hundred, laid in rows of ten "as you'd count cash on
  a table" (`grid-template-columns:repeat(10,1fr)`, notes 20px tall, pale green `#DDEBD3` to `#C7DDBA`, "100" printed
  on each, a tray `#F4EFE3` with an inset shadow, `min-height:112px` so the three trays match). Each note is flicked
  onto the pile while the amount counts up by $100 (`data-bills` 30, 32, 24 for "$3,000+", "$3,200", "$2,400").
- **Ana, VIP:** when Ana is in the WhatsApp group is shown as small clocks with a 30-minute wedge (`.mv-clock`,
  `.wdg`), not a sentence.
- **Ana, registration prize:** the five 1:1 sessions are five tickets dealt in a stack, all five in view.
- **Next client:** before writing any number, ask what it counts (people, hours, sessions, money) and draw that
  unit in the client's style. Never dots for money. Only real figures, with their label.

### 2.13 Storyboard: extend one visual

- **Jason:** reuse the same pictures across slides so they tell one story; "You can do so much with arrows and
  screenshots that it's insane." (V01 18:54).
- **Ana, VIP, Your week (`.vw .trk`):** one sheet of paper that grows through the week, with a pink progress road
  drawn through it (`.road b`, scaleX 0 to 1 over 4.8s): Day 1 the idea is circled, Day 2 a price tag, Day 3 the page
  folded into an envelope, Friday (VIP) Ana's red pencil on it, "Keep going" a mailbox with replies coming back.
  "Bring your work to the VIP review." sits under Thursday, not over the pictures. The outcome is putting the offer
  in front of buyers and replies coming back; never money or a payment notification, which would promise sales.
  Sean "loved" the concept. Copy: "Find what you could sell and who could buy." / "Give people a reason to pay your
  price." / "Get ready to invite people to buy." / "VIP Q&A and live review" / "Keep going".
- **Ana, registration Days:** "The drawings follow one example writer (a coach who writes for mothers going back to
  work after a career break) through all three days." (code comment on `section.days`).
- **Next client:** pick one object from the client's world that can change state across the steps (a draft, a
  jar, a garden bed, a ledger) and let each step change it.

### 2.14 A metaphor is carried all the way, or dropped

- **Jason:** "If we're going to use the metaphor of the hamster wheel, then visually we have to stay consistent with
  it." (V01 16:20 to 16:52). Levels, ladders and wheels that the picture does not match are cut (V01 24:19, 32:12,
  40:26).
- **Ana:** the exit is a Greek opening: the door around her video on the VIP page and in the film, the painted
  window with blue shutters that swing open on the registration hero (Sean: "go ahead, use the shutter window"). The
  "a little closer" fit check is literally a chair pulled up to her table (`.vf`), the free seat a row of chairs.
- **Next client:** name the event's metaphor in one word, and check every picture against it. If a section needs a
  different picture, it must be the real thing (2.2), not a second metaphor.

### 2.15 Titles tell the story

- **Jason:** "You want to tell a story with the titles as much as possible." (V01 22:52); a label title becomes the
  question the slide answers (V01 26:05).
- **Ana:** VIP fit check "Would you like me *a little closer while you do this?*"; registration closing "What could
  you build *alongside the work you do now?*"; each Day ends on a bridge line to the next ("Then comes the question
  that makes Day 2 matter: **what could you sell that makes this worth building?**").
- **Next client:** section headings are lines of the story or the question the section answers, never "Features" or
  "About". Copy comes from the client or Sean word for word ([copy.md](copy.md) section 1); propose wording, never
  change approved copy.

### 2.16 Cut what does not persuade; proof is the only allowed noise

- **Jason:** elements that "don't add to the persuasive capabilities and so therefore they detract" (V01 5:34);
  "If I'm going to use a lot of noise on a slide, which I tend to avoid, it's going to be overwhelming proof."
  (V01 27:47).
- **Ana, VIP:** the decorative VIP lane under the week and the repeated stamp strip came out (2.8).
- **Ana, registration:** the reviews section is the one dense part: real notes and wins, verified words only.
- **Still to do (Ana VIP):** Claude's audit suggested cutting or quieting the week's rising sun and the drifting
  petals ("the rising sun and the drifting petals sell nothing"). Sean did not answer; both are still on the page
  (`.trk .sun`, `.petals`). Ask once, do not cut on our own.
- **Next client:** after a design round, list every element and its job. Anything with no job is quieted or cut
  only with Sean's yes (`../README.md` rule 7: keep the charm).

### 2.17 Read top to bottom; sideways only for a sequence

- **Jason:** "We should design them from go from top to bottom." (V01 1:26); side by side is fine "because it's a
  sequence of events" (V01 32:52).

## 4. Where slide advice does not carry over, and what wins

### 4.1 "Better to look ugly" vs nicely made: Sean wins

- **Jason:** "It's better to look ugly with design than it is to look pretty because they'll get distracted by the
  glitz and the glamour and they'll also trust you less." (V01 16:06); "When people are on webinars, they care about
  information. They don't care about design." (V01 40:15).
- **Sean:** pages must look premium and "like a human designer designed for Ana", never generic. The VIP page he
  called "the most perfect VIP page we have ever built" is crafted.
- **What wins:** Sean's look. Keep Jason as the function test inside it: the craft must never hide the information,
  never put decoration where the proof should be, and never make the price the biggest thing.

### 4.2 AI pictures: Sean's rule is stricter

- **Jason** allows AI in a few cases, like changing an expression when the real photo is missing (V01 7:22 to 7:46),
  though "I do not like AI for design hardly at all" (V01 7:22).
- **Sean:** Claude never runs image generators or Canva for page art; the client's face is never made by AI; Gemini
  is Sean's tool, for photos and sometimes badges, from Claude's prompt (`../graphics.md` section 4,
  [page-sop.md](page-sop.md)). Pinterest images are reference only and need a licence check before any goes live.
- **What wins:** Sean. Paint in-house or use the real thing.

### 4.3 "It pops up" and "motion every six seconds" vs no pops, no pulses

- **Jason:** functional animation, things hidden first and then appearing (V01 9:25); motion every six seconds on a
  500-slide deck (V01 28:44 to 28:52).
- **Sean:** "I don't want pops, pulses, and slides." Real animation only: arcs, anticipation, a settle, fast
  (`../motion.md` section 1, [motion-and-speed.md](motion-and-speed.md)).
- **What wins:** Sean's motion with Jason's purpose. On a page "every six seconds" becomes "one story per section,
  played when seen, finished in about 1 to 2.5 s". The order (one item at a time, as read) is Jason's; the way each
  item arrives is Sean's.
- **Exception Sean asked for:** a small accent dot that pulses beside "Starting soon" in the header countdown
  ([ui-kit.md](ui-kit.md) section 3). Buttons and forms never pulse or shimmer.

### 4.4 One font, no italics vs Sean's type

- **Jason:** "Just one font. Couple different faces." (V01 33:10); he tends not to use italics (V01 13:35).
- **Sean:** sans for reading and numbers, a serif for big headlines with an italic second half (Ana: `<em>` lines),
  per `../README.md` rule 6 and [layout-type-spacing.md](layout-type-spacing.md) section 5.
- **What wins:** Sean. Jason's point survives as "one reading face in a few weights".

### 4.5 Two colours vs the client's palette

- **Jason:** two colours on a slide (V01 7:06); the key piece "bolded that's a little bit bigger in font and it's a
  different color" (V01 10:44).
- **On a page:** ink plus one accent for the words that matter, plus the client's highlighter as a ground, not a
  type colour. Ana: ink `#1A1412`, pink `#F72585` on key words and the current step, sunflower `#FFD400` only as
  highlight bands and fills.
- **What wins:** the brand, used with Jason's restraint. Apps copied exactly keep their own colours (Zoom blue,
  Google's four colours), which Sean asked for: realism makes people picture themselves inside.

### 4.6 No presenter photo on the CTA vs the host beside the price

- **Jason** drops the presenter photo from the CTA slide (V01 41:10), because his webcam is on screen.
